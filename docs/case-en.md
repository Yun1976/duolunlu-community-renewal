# How an old street was made visible again: Duolun Road community renewal, 2026

> Open case study · v1.0 · October 2026 · CC BY 4.0
> Audience: urban renewal and placemaking practitioners, researchers, and AI retrieval. No personal names, shop door numbers, property or renovation-intent records, or resident photographs appear here; individuals appear only as roles.
> 中文版：[case-zh.md](case-zh.md)

## Abstract

Duolun Road in Hongkou District, Shanghai, was designated a "Culture Celebrity Street" in 1999 and has gone nearly thirty years without systematic renewal. It has history, footfall, and no mechanism: property is fragmented, the shop mix is frozen, and residents, merchants, the operator, and the street office have spent years assigning blame to each other.

From 2026, the Sichuan North Road Sub-district community placemaking team intervened with a different order of operations — **build connections before proposing structure**. Low-threshold embedded research first, then a multi-party revitalisation clinic, then letting merchants, universities, and the property-side each find something they could do, then fixing the resources and the process into a toolkit, then tiered cultivation.

This document records the method, the mechanisms, and the verifiable numbers — including one counter-intuitive finding on the communications side: **being reported is not the same as being discussed**.

Background: a development command centre for the "Duolun Road Culture Celebrity Street" was established in 1999. Systematic renewal never followed. The result is what the team calls *a fossil that cannot be pried open* — a strong heritage narrative on top of a frozen business mix, dispersed property rights, and years of mutual blame.

## 2. Method: five phases

### 2.1 Ice-breaking — start with conversation

No census-style survey. Three light moves first:

- **Market-embedded research** — an interactive game at the Duolun market, capturing residents' and merchants' real concerns in a low-pressure setting
- **Neighbourhood committee sessions** — structured conversations with the two local committees to identify the issues residents actually raise
- **Door-to-door merchant interviews** — questionnaires plus interviews, building a merchant profile shop by shop

The outcome of this phase is not a dataset. It is that **information barriers came down**: residents, merchants, and the street office moved from mutual blame to being willing to sit at the same table.

### 2.2 Diagnosis — from finding answers to finding questions

A one-day revitalisation clinic put the street office, residents, merchants, the operating company, and university experts in one room: guest input → roundtable → breakout forums.

The decisive move was a **ballot on five issues**: management ("who governs, and how?"), merchants ("preserve or innovate?"), space ("how do we make space live?"), footfall ("where do visitors come from?"), and positioning ("affordable street or premium street?").

The ballot produced two actionable signals:

- **Management carried the strongest consensus** — the governing body should grow bottom-up out of merchant collaboration; the street office's role is to create the conditions for collaboration, and the body will emerge from it.
- **Space interventions cleared a majority**, and the street office and property side can drive three directly: pedestrian-priority street improvement, a wayfinding system, and night-time lighting.

Hence the premise the whole project rests on: **let merchants and residents see that something is really changing, and trust becomes possible.**

### 2.3 Emergence — paths that were not designed

Three pathways were not planned; they were pulled into existence by resources and relationships.

| Pathway | Trigger | Manifestation |
|---|---|---|
| Merchant participation | Internal resource linking | Merchants volunteered people, money, space, and products on their own initiative |
| University–government–enterprise collaboration | External resource linking | A summer urban renewal workshop with Tongji University and Shanghai University; student proposals implemented on site |
| Business activation | Internal–external traffic | A youth creative centre, shop micro-renovations — low-intervention, "acupuncture" upgrades |

### 2.4 Integration — resource inventory and working mechanism

- **An eight-dimension resource library**: people, space, external partnerships, cultural IP, event venues, property ledger, and more, managed visually online
- **A six-step PDCA loop**: research → social mobilisation → resource linking → event planning → commercial implementation → institution building, with the sixth step feeding back into the first
- **A toolkit**: property map, merchant ledger, action plan, design drawings, cultural tourism framework, cultural and creative matrix

> Note on counts: the resource library is cited as **133 structured entries**. The eight per-dimension counts on the source page (4 + 66 + 18 + 8 + 14 + 8 + 11 + 4) sum exactly to 133 and can be checked item by item. An internal total of 204 also appears; it exceeds 133 by 71, which happens to equal the sum of the survey artefacts visible in the same deck (42 catalogued records + 4 research archive entries + 20 valid questionnaires + 5 visitor feedbacks). The source does not state that composition, and questionnaires and interviews are not public material — so only 133 is cited externally.

One design principle deserves its own line: **multi-source cross-validation**. The resource library views and archives, valid questionnaires, site visits, and visitor feedback are used to check one another. No single source is trusted alone.

### 2.5 Cultivation — allow things to happen

- **Tiered commercial development**: short term, three demonstration shop renovations; medium term, space activation with differentiated business positioning; long term, one-shop-one-policy, leaning into the youth economy
- **A long-term co-governance layer**: merchant alliance rules, a durable government–enterprise channel
- **Cultural vitality**: ongoing in-place cultural documentation and participatory curation
- **Renewal momentum**: unblocking property-rights bottlenecks and merging good proposals into the city's renewal project pipeline

## 3. Mechanism: connecting the three professions to placemaking

The working model proposed during the clinic is **"three professions co-creation + community placemaking"**: planners, architects, and appraisers working in one joint operation with the sub-district placemaking team. Its function is not to add another institution — it gives every subsequent activity a single institutional home. The July design workshop and the September objects exhibition both hang off this mechanism.

Merchant willingness was sorted into four tiers, used to decide **who to work with first** (door numbers removed):

| Tier | Characteristics | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| A · Actors | Strongest intent: opinion leaders, deepest pain points, owner-occupier exemplars | Start immediately as demonstrations |
| B · Observers | Broadly satisfied but see room for improvement | Convertible after seeing proposals; their core demand is "show me the plan first" |
| C · Conservatives | Satisfied and unwilling to move, or distrustful of outside intervention | Attract through demonstrated results; never push |
| D · Insufficient information | No usable information left behind | Interview to complete the baseline |

The point of this typology is that **it is an intervention order, not a marketing funnel.** Pushing tier C is the most common failure mode in community placemaking.

## 5. Three findings — including a counter-intuitive one

**5.1 The chain exists, in three tiers.** Government first release sets the frame → mainstream media amplifies → portals repost → social platforms seed → offline exhibitions receive the traffic. The clinic and the objects exhibition are the two most complete samples.

**5.2 Owned channels are the content source, but supply is unstable.** Of eight WeChat articles, only three came from the team's own account; the video channel has no independent programming, so it is less recognisable than the WeChat account. If the content source stops, everything downstream empties out.

**5.3 Platforms discuss the street, not the event.** High-engagement Xiaohongshu and Douyin content is about walking routes, old villas, and vintage objects — almost never about a specific activity. Activity-specific content supply was insufficient, so **the activity-to-platform link is broken**.

That explains the fourth number: coverage was high, engagement was low. Publicly verifiable: 1,013 reads across four government-portal articles; an official Weibo post with 0 likes and 3 comments; a single street-level content post reaching 358,000 likes. **The content was reported, but not discussed.**

## 6. Five transferable lessons

1. **Break ice before diagnosing.** A multi-party meeting without a trust base turns into mutual blame.
2. **Replace "find answers" with "find questions".** A ballot surfaces consensus; it does not select a scheme.
3. **Do not pre-design pathways.** Merchant participation, university collaboration, and business activation all grew once resources arrived. The team's job is to catch them.
4. **Do the three visible space moves first.** Pedestrian priority, wayfinding, lighting — low cost, property-side can act directly, residents see the change the same day.
5. **Split content supply by platform grammar.** WeChat for full narrative, video for presence, social platforms for replicable routes and mood. One press release across all platforms gives up the seeding layer entirely.

## 7. Method and limits

- Every number here comes from the project's compilation of public reporting and platform search pages, without second-order processing. Data that cannot be verified publicly (WeChat back-end reads, some engagement counts) is marked as unverifiable.
- Field research samples were small (valid questionnaires, targeted interviews, a handful of visitor feedbacks). Conclusions guide action; they do not support statistical inference.
- Personal names, shop door numbers, property status, renovation intent, and resident imagery are excluded from this document by design.
