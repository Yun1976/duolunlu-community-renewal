Duolun Road (多伦路, Duōlún Lù) is a short L-shaped street in Shanghai's Hongkou District, a few minutes' walk from Lu Xun Park and Hongkou Stadium. Both of its ends meet North Sichuan Road (四川北路), and the street itself has been closed to traffic and given over entirely to pedestrians since 1999. In the 1920s and 1930s it was one of the most electrifying addresses in Chinese letters: Lu Xun, Guo Moruo, Mao Dun, Ye Shengtao and Ding Ling all lived in its lanes, and the League of Left-Wing Writers was founded here in 1930. Today it is a quiet, browsable street of galleries, antique shops, bookstores, cafés and studios — a place where the Republican-era past and the working present of a Shanghai neighbourhood sit side by side.

This site is an independent English-language guide to the street: its history, its landmarks, its shops and galleries, its seasonal cultural life, and everything you need to plan a visit. Every fact on it is drawn from documented sources — see our editorial policy.

Why this street matters为什么是多伦路

In the space of one short street, the whole drama of modern Shanghai plays out: colonial extra-settlement roads, stone-gate lane houses, church architecture, the golden age of Chinese literature, and a living neighbourhood that never stopped being one.

A literary crossroads

The League of Left-Wing Writers was founded on this street in 1930, at the former campus of China Art University. Lu Xun lived around the corner in Jingyun Li, where his lamp burned late over essays and translations.

An open-air architecture museum

Twenty-five protected historic buildings line the street: a Chinese-Western fusion church from 1928, an Islamic-style mansion, French neoclassical residences, and dense lanes of shikumen housing — a cross-section of 20th-century Shanghai in 550 metres.

A street that still works

Unlike many preserved old streets, Duolun Road is not a stage set. Real galleries, guqin makers, bookshops, cafés and family businesses trade here, and the neighbourhood's cultural programmes run through all four seasons.
