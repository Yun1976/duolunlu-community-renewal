A letter from the street来自川北的一封信

Streets keep their own archives, in people. This letter — adapted and translated from the neighbourhood's own record, with names withheld for privacy — describes an afternoon when one of the street's memory-keepers shared what he had spent a lifetime collecting.

The day's emotion, I hardly know where to begin writing down.

Time is an invisible brush, and one of its strokes is nostalgia. When the local historian — seventy years old, born with the new republic, still quick and warm in speech — began to talk, what poured out was not a lecture but an inheritance: his schooldays in Hongkou, the neighbours of these lanes, the photographs and documents he and a friend had gathered over years, the poems they wrote about all of it. Anecdotes of famous names whose addresses stood one door from where we were sitting.

Fragments of the old hours spread themselves out slowly, and the street — bustling once, quiet for long since — settled, for an afternoon, into one long river, fished up again from the depths of the years by hands and eyes that treasured it, re-coloured, glowing.

Time is also a visible force, and one of its threads is expectation. He told, unhurried, of the attempts he and his friends had made over the years to make this home ground better known. Affection summons affection; shared purpose wakes shared purpose. Those experiences and those feelings finally condensed into pieces of writing and into letters — carefully composed, carefully copied out — delivered, as he wished, into the hands of people who would listen, and be moved.

We hope this feeling can keep fermenting, in more dimensions and more forms, and echo in the hearts of the many people who care about this warm ground of the city.

They were fished up from the depths of the years and from the fire and smoke of daily life by the hands and eyes that treasured them — re-coloured, glowing. 从岁月深处与烟火人间打捞起来，重新着色，荣光焕发

Why letters

The neighbourhood around Duolun Road has a habit of writing to itself: letters to the street, letters carried along an 800-metre "post road", letters written to the future. In a district whose literary past was made of essays, pamphlets and correspondence — Lu Xun's own letters among the most-read in the language — the letter is the street's native literary form. This page will carry further letters as the archive grows.
