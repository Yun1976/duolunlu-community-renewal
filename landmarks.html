Landmarks · 地标

Nine stops, one walk九处看点，一次走完

Everything below sits on — or a few steps off — a street you can walk end to end in eight minutes. Give it two hours and it will give you back a century.

No. 27MUSEUM

Duolun Museum of Modern Art

27 Duolun Road · 多伦现代美术馆

Shanghai's museum of modern and contemporary art on a historic street — part of the 1998–99 regeneration project, with rotating exhibitions across several floors.

No. 591928 · ARCHITECTURE

Hongde Tang (Fitch Memorial Church)

59 Duolun Road · 鸿德堂

A rare surviving Chinese-Western fusion church: a Protestant congregation housed in a building that looks like a classical Chinese hall, completed in 1928 and named after missionary George Field Fitch. The only church of this mixed style surviving in Shanghai.

No. 119STREET FURNITURE

Xishi Bell Tower

119 Duolun Road · 夕拾钟楼

The street's landmark clock tower, named for Lu Xun's essay collection Dawn Blossoms Plucked at Dusk (朝花夕拾). A meeting point and a reminder that this street keeps literary time.

No. 123CAFÉ · 1920s VILLA

Old Film Café

123 Duolun Road · 老电影咖啡馆

A café inside a two-storey 1920s colonial veranda-style villa (listed as a protected historic building in 2013), screening classic films from the silent era through the 1940s. Tea, coffee, and the flicker of old Shanghai.

Lane 135LANE · HISTORY

Jingyun Li

135 Duolun Road (lane) · 景云里

The stone-gate lane where Lu Xun and Mao Dun lived in the late 1920s–30s. A classic Shanghai shikumen lane, still recognisably a working neighbourhood — walk it slowly and the geometry of the writers' daily life is still there.

No. 145MEMORIAL

League of Left-Wing Writers Memorial Hall

145 Duolun Road · 中国左翼作家联盟成立大会会址

At the former campus of China Art University, the League of Left-Wing Writers was founded on 2 March 1930. The memorial hall preserves the founding meeting site and tells the story of the movement and its writers.

No. 210MANSION

The No. 210 Mansion

210 Duolun Road · 白公馆

A substantial Republican-era residence known locally as the "White Mansion", home for a time to general Bai Chongxi — and where his son, the novelist Pai Hsien-yung, spent part of his childhood.

No. 250MANSION

Kung Residence

250 Duolun Road · 孔公馆

The street's architectural exotic: an Islamic-style mansion with a dome and horseshoe arches, built for the financier H.H. Kung. Look up — the detail is in the roofline.

2 min awayNEARBY

Uchiyama Bookstore site

2050 North Sichuan Road · 内山书店旧址

A short walk off the street: the former bookstore of Japanese bookseller Uchiyama Kanzō, Lu Xun's friend and bookseller. One of the most important bookish addresses in modern Chinese literary history.

House numbers follow current street records and public listings; memorial plaques along the street document who lived where. Twenty-five protected historic buildings stand in the street and its surrounding blocks — the nine above are the ones most visitors shouldn't miss.
