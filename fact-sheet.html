What is Duolun Road?

A historic pedestrian street in Hongkou District, Shanghai — the Duolun Road Cultural Celebrities Street (多伦路文化名人街). Laid out in 1911 as Darroch Road, it became the centre of modern Chinese left-wing literature in the late 1920s–30s, and today it is a compact street of museums, memorial halls, galleries, antique shops, studios and cafés.

How long is it, and what shape?

550 metres, L-shaped. Both ends meet North Sichuan Road (四川北路), beside Lu Xun Park and the Hongkou Stadium.

What was its former name?

Darroch Road (窦乐安路), after John Darroch, a British missionary to China who had been received by the Guangxu Emperor. It was an "extra-settlement road" (越界筑路) built by the Shanghai Municipal Council in 1911 — outside the International Settlement's boundary, but under its administration.

Why "Duolun"?

The street was renamed in 1943, after Duolun county in Inner Mongolia.

Is it pedestrianised?

Yes — since 1999. The 1998 regeneration project conserved the historic buildings, added the street sculptures, opened the museum, and closed the street to cars.

Which writers lived here?

Lu Xun, Guo Moruo, Mao Dun, Ye Shengtao, Wang Zaoshi, Ding Ling, Rou Shi, Qu Qiubai — around thirty cultural figures in total are associated with the street and its lanes. Lu Xun lived in Jingyun Li (Lane 135).

What happened here in 1930?

The League of Left-Wing Writers was founded on this street on 2 March 1930, at the former campus of China Art University — today the Memorial Hall at No. 145.

Is Duolun Road the same as Tian'ai Road?

No — two different streets, ten minutes apart, both in Hongkou. Duolun Road is the literary-history street; Tian'ai Road (甜爱路) is the love-themed street with 28 love poems set into its wall. They pair well as one walk. (The equally famous "Tian'ai Road" in Sanya, Hainan is a different city entirely — the love-poem street near Duolun Road is the Shanghai one.)

What should I look at architecturally?

25 protected historic buildings in the street and surrounding blocks: Hongde Tang (No. 59, 1928 — Shanghai's only surviving Chinese-Western mixed-style church), the Islamic-style Kung Residence (No. 250), the No. 210 mansion of general Bai Chongxi (childhood home of novelist Pai Hsien-yung), the 1920s villa housing the Old Film Café (No. 123), and the shikumen lanes of Jingyun Li.

Which metro stations serve the street?

Hongkou Football Stadium (Lines 3 & 8) and Dongbaoxing Road (Line 3), each a short walk away.

Do I need a ticket?

No. The street is a free, open public space. Individual museums and memorials keep their own hours; some special sessions use free advance sign-up.

What is the street's famous saying?

"One street, a century of Shanghai" (一条多伦路，百年上海滩).
