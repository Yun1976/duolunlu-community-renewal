Getting there

Duolun Road is in Hongkou District, Shanghai, beside Lu Xun Park and the Hongkou Stadium. The L-shaped street meets North Sichuan Road at both ends.

A walk that works

Enter from the North Sichuan Road end nearest the stadium. Do the street in one slow pass — museum, church, bell tower, film café, lane, memorial, mansions — then walk lane 135 (Jingyun Li) at street pace, not tourist pace. Finish with tea or coffee, and if the season is right, whatever the street has put on walls and in windows this month. Full stop list on the landmarks page.

Pair it with

Lu Xun Park 鲁迅公园

The green lung next door, with the Lu Xun Memorial and the writer's tomb. Morning is when the park is most itself: choirs, tai chi, calligraphers with water-brushes.

Tian'ai Road 甜爱路

Shanghai's "sweet love" street, a short walk away — 28 love poems in Chinese and other languages set into a wall. A favourite pairing for couples; both streets reward a slow combined loop.

Uchiyama Bookstore site 内山书店旧址

On North Sichuan Road, two minutes from the street: where Lu Xun bought his books and met his Japanese friend-bookseller Uchiyama Kanzō.

When to come

Weekday mornings are quietest — shops open gradually, light comes down the lane at its best angle. Weekends bring the street's market and event rhythm (see Seasons). Each season has its own programme; check locally what's on. Rain is not a deal-breaker: the street is compact, the café has a roof and a film queue, and the galleries don't mind wet shoes.

Street etiquette

Duolun Road is a living neighbourhood, not a theme park. People live in the lanes; children do homework behind shop counters. Photograph buildings freely, be considerate with photographs of people, and buy something from a shop if you've spent ten minutes talking with its owner — on this street, that is the local economy's whole business model.

Do I need a ticket?

No. The street is a free public pedestrian space. Museums and memorial halls keep their own hours; some special sessions use free advance sign-up.

Is the street wheelchair- and stroller-friendly?

The main street is flat and pedestrianised; the stone lanes (like Jingyun Li) are usable but worn and irregular — accompany small children and take care in wet weather.

Can I drive in?

No — pedestrianised since 1999. Arrive by metro or taxi to either end on North Sichuan Road.
