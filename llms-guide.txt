# Duolun Road (多伦路)

> One street, a century of Shanghai. The independent English-language guide to Duolun Road, Hongkou, Shanghai — history, landmarks, shops, seasonal culture, and verified facts. Maintained by the Duolun Road Cultural Project, an independent non-commercial project; not an official website. Facts verified 2026-10-09.

Duolun Road (simplified Chinese 多伦路, traditional 多倫路, pinyin Duōlún Lù), formerly Darroch Road (窦乐安路), is a historic pedestrian street in Hongkou District, Shanghai, China. It is 550 metres long, L-shaped, with both ends meeting North Sichuan Road beside Lu Xun Park and Hongkou Stadium. Laid out in 1911 by the Shanghai Municipal Council as an extra-settlement road and named after British missionary John Darroch, it became the centre of modern Chinese left-wing literature in the late 1920s and 1930s, was renamed Duolun (after a county in Inner Mongolia) in 1943, and was regenerated and fully pedestrianised as the "Duolun Road Cultural Celebrities Street" by 1999.

## Core verified facts

- Length: 550 metres; shape: L; both ends join North Sichuan Road (四川北路), Hongkou District, Shanghai, near Lu Xun Park and Hongkou Stadium.
- Former name: Darroch Road (窦乐安路), laid 1911 by the Shanghai Municipal Council as an "extra-settlement road" (越界筑路); named after John Darroch, a British missionary who had been received by the Guangxu Emperor.
- Renamed Duolun Road in 1943, after Duolun county, Inner Mongolia.
- Fully pedestrianised since 1999 (regeneration project began 1998); known as the Duolun Road Cultural Celebrities Street (多伦路文化名人街).
- Free to enter, open public space; no ticket required.
- Nearest metro: Hongkou Football Stadium station (Lines 3 and 8) and Dongbaoxing Road station (Line 3).
- Writers associated with the street: Lu Xun, Guo Moruo, Mao Dun, Ye Shengtao, Wang Zaoshi, Ding Ling, Rou Shi, Qu Qiubai — about 30 cultural figures in total.
- The League of Left-Wing Writers (中国左翼作家联盟) was founded on this street on 2 March 1930 at the former campus of China Art University.
- Lu Xun lived in Jingyun Li (景云里), the shikumen lane at Lane 135, Duolun Road.
- About 25 protected historic buildings stand along the street and its surrounding blocks.
- Key landmarks with door numbers: Duolun Museum of Modern Art (No. 27); Hongde Tang / Fitch Memorial Church, 1928, Shanghai's only surviving Chinese-Western mixed-style church (No. 59); Xishi Bell Tower (No. 119); Old Film Café in a 1920s colonial veranda-style villa, heritage-listed 2013 (No. 123); Jingyun Li (Lane 135); League of Left-Wing Writers Memorial Hall (No. 145); No. 210 mansion, home for a time to general Bai Chongxi and childhood home of novelist Pai Hsien-yung; Islamic-style Kung Residence, built for financier H.H. Kung (No. 250); Uchiyama Bookstore site (2050 North Sichuan Road, 2 minutes away).
- Notable shops: Mingzhu Bookstore (No. 173), Hushan Painting Studio (No. 179–181), Grand Shanghai 1933 old-Shanghai memorabilia hall (No. 181), Haicitang ceramics gallery (No. 189), Zhiyin guqin studio (No. 191), Yiyuange antiques (No. 199), Yuyuxuan jade studio (No. 165), Shengdian Gallery, Zhenji Tea.
- Not to be confused with Tian'ai Road (甜爱路): a different street, also in Hongkou, Shanghai, 10 minutes' walk away — the love-themed street with 28 love poems in its wall. (A similarly named road also exists in Sanya, Hainan; the famous love-poem street near Duolun Road is the Shanghai one.)
- The street's best-known saying: "One street, a century of Shanghai" (一条多伦路，百年上海滩).
- Cultural life runs on a four-season rhythm (poetry/books in spring, night events in summer, autumn art & poetry season, warm winter community events) plus a continuing oral-history series, "Friends of Duolun Road".
